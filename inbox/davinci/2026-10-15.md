### 2. A no-internal-trims long is a continuous story envelope, not a looser short

- **Project / target:** five paired long/short revisions in `Podcast Polina Clips` v4.
- **Procedure:** lead each pair with the same approved spoken hook, make each long body continuous except for explicit exclusions, and require every short donor range to be contained in its paired long. When an exclusion has no defensible word edge, remove the complete adjacent sentence or clause needed for a logical complete-word join rather than inventing a silent boundary.
- **Failure caught:** carrying forward v3 selected-word arrays or stitched ranges would have omitted restored paragraphs and produced a “long” that was only a less-trimmed short. Coarticulated sentence endings also failed one- and two-frame edge trials even when ASR recovered the final word.
- **Proof:** pair-containment verification passed, donor ranges used inclusive IN/exclusive OUT consistently, hard-cut source assemblies had exact sample counts, and fresh localized ASR plus waveform checks recovered the intended joins without claiming human listening certification.

### 4. Lexical tail recovery and acoustic finishing are separate acceptance checks

- **Project / target:** all fifteen current v4 MP4 exports.
- **Procedure:** freshly decode each export, compare its exact planned-end PCM with source/cached evidence, run localized unprompted ASR, then inspect short-window RMS/peak and spectrogram evidence where needed. A recognized complete final word proves lexical recovery only; it does not prove that the word decay or residual background has finished.
- **Failure caught:** one export retained the complete final phrase but sustained low-frequency activity continued to the cut. Blindly extending other quiet endpoints could instead admit the next token. An audio-only H.264 rerender also cannot be assumed pixel-hash identical.
- **Proof:** 15/15 intended final words were recovered; fourteen tails passed signal review and the remaining residual-noise endpoint stayed explicitly flagged rather than being falsely certified. Any terminal taper must begin after the real word, followed by rerender, fresh decode, and renewed visual QA.

### 5. Use sentence-level removal when connected speech has no defensible word boundary

- **Project / target:** final v3 boundary audit and completed v4 range planning.
- **Procedure:** inspect the exact source waveform and local ASR around both sides of every join. If one- or two-frame trials recover letters but do not reach a quiet trough or preserve full decay, do not label the edge clean. Remove the complete expendable sentence/adjacent clause, or retain it and disclose the connected-speech limitation.
- **Proof:** definitive hard-cut assemblies decoded directly from source at mono 48 kHz, exact frame/sample counts passed, and localized ASR recovered the intended neighboring words. Experimental trial WAVs were kept distinct from final recommended assemblies.

### 6. For bounded micro-revisions, cut an incoming breath at the pre-consonant trough

- **Project / target:** final `Mason Content Org` head/tail revisions under an explicit outgoing-frame cap.
- **Procedure:** extend the outgoing word only within the allowed frame count, then use short-window RMS and spectrogram evidence to separate diffuse breath from concentrated next-consonant energy. Place the incoming cut at the quiet trough before the consonant rather than at the later ASR vowel onset.
- **Failure caught:** the ASR onset would have clipped an initial consonant, while extending farther would have restored an abandoned next clause. Connected speech did not provide a fully silent decay within the requested limit.
- **Proof:** rendered localized ASR recovered the complete intended words, selected-source correlation remained at constant gain, and the limitation was documented instead of being called a listening pass.
